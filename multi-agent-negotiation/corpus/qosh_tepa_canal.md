Construction of the Qosh Tepa canal in northern Afghanistan began on 30 March 2022, after the Taliban regained power in 2021. The planned length is 285 km. As of a January 2025 report, almost half of the canal was complete. The canal is due to be fully operational by 2028.

The canal could divert as much as 10 cubic km of water every year from the Amu Darya. The irrigation target is to convert about 550,000 hectares of arid land in northern Afghanistan into farmland and to employ about a quarter million people.

About 30 percent of the Amu Darya's flow originates in the Afghan mountains. Afghanistan currently uses about 2 cubic km of water a year, which is about eleven times less than Uzbekistan and five times less than Turkmenistan.

There is no water use agreement between Afghanistan and the Central Asian states. Central Asian governments do not officially recognize the Taliban as Afghanistan's legitimate rulers, although Taliban officials attend regional events. The Taliban say they are open to dialogue but treat the canal as a domestic issue. Analysts note that no one disputes Afghanistan's right to use Amu Darya water.

A section of the canal wall collapsed shortly after the first part opened in 2023, creating an artificial lake of over 30 square km. Without concrete lining, the canal may lose large amounts of water to sandy soil.

Some studies estimate that within five to six years of operation, average water intake by Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan in the middle and lower reaches could fall from 80 percent to 65 percent of current levels.
