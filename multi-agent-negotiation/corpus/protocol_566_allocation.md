Protocol 566 was signed on 10 September 1987 by the Soviet water authorities. It set a total annual limit of 61.5 bcm (km3) of Amu Darya water and divided it among the Soviet republics. Uzbekistan received 29.6 bcm (48.2 percent). Turkmenistan received 22.0 bcm (35.8 percent). Tajikistan received 9.5 bcm (15.4 percent). Kyrgyzstan received 0.4 bcm (0.6 percent).

Protocol 566 assumed that Afghanistan would divert 2.1 bcm per year, but Kabul does not appear to have been consulted. Kazakhstan was excluded from the Amu Darya allocation.

After independence, the five Central Asian republics kept the Soviet era shares through the 1992 Almaty Agreement, which created the Interstate Commission for Water Coordination (ICWC). Afghanistan was not a party to the Almaty Agreement and is excluded from the ICWC framework.

Amu Darya waters make up about 80 percent of all accessible water resources in the region. Average river inflow to the Aral Sea over the last 50 years has dropped to about 12.7 cubic km, almost 4.5 times less than before large scale irrigation.
