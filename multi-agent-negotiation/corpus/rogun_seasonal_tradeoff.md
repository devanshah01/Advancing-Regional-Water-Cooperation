The Rogun dam is on the Vakhsh River, a tributary of the Amu Darya, in Tajikistan. Construction began in 1976 and was delayed by funding gaps, floods and civil war. Completion is planned for 2028.

Tajikistan treats hydropower as essential to energy security. Around 70 percent of the country regularly faces electricity shortages, and the winter of 2007 to 2008 caused significant loss of life and livestock, mainly from energy shortages.

The core conflict is seasonal. Tajikistan wants to release stored water in winter, when its electricity demand peaks. Uzbekistan needs water released in summer for irrigation. Storing water in summer for winter power generation reduces irrigation water downstream during the growing season.

Only about 10 percent of Uzbekistan's total river runoff forms within its own territory. Cotton accounted for about USD 1.3 billion, around 13 percent of total export value, in 2019.

The USSR built dams so upstream republics could store water and release it for downstream irrigation, with energy supplied to upstream republics in return. The ICWC, created in 1992, continued the Soviet water sharing but did not cover energy supplies to upstream states. A 1998 water and energy exchange deal in the region collapsed in 2002 over electricity prices.

Relations improved after 2016 under Uzbek President Mirziyoyev, and Uzbekistan became less vehemently opposed to Rogun. In 2021 the two countries agreed to jointly build two hydroelectric plants on the Zarafshan River in Tajikistan.
