In Uzbekistan, about 40 percent of the population depends directly or indirectly on agriculture, and cotton has contributed around 17 percent of GDP. In Turkmenistan, agriculture accounts for about 10 percent of GDP and employs nearly half of the workforce. Both countries rely on water intensive cotton and neither has shown readiness to stop growing it.

Turkmenistan draws Amu Darya water through the Karakum Canal. Uzbekistan's lower reaches, including Karakalpakstan and Khorezm near the Aral Sea delta, are the most exposed to shortfalls, since they sit at the end of the river.

Uzbekistan has invested in Afghan energy and power lines, and Turkmenistan has invested in gas pipeline construction through Afghanistan, which gives both downstream states economic links with Kabul beyond water.

Tajikistan experienced water shortages in summer 2025, and Kyrgyzstan and Kazakhstan experienced disputes over water in 2023.
