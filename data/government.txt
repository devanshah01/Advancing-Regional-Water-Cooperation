[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 1A: Uzbekistan Stance & Lines]
Uzbekistan (downstream agricultural giant; leads IFAS) treats water scarcity as a national security and economic threat to Khorezm and Karakalpakstan jobs. As a tail-end user, it views Afghanistan's uncoordinated Qosh Tepa Canal as a major water threat and favors regional integration and digitalization.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 1B: Uzbekistan OSCE Priorities]
For OSCE advisory work, Uzbekistan prioritizes irrigation and pumping modernization, automated water metering, canal lining, and bringing Afghanistan into formal transboundary water dialogue.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 2A: Turkmenistan Stance & Lines]
Turkmenistan protects state cotton and wheat quotas and favors quiet bilateral water diplomacy over public disputes. Heavily reliant on the leaky Karakum Canal across sandy desert, it opposes external mandates that dictate domestic volumetric cuts to its agricultural targets.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 2B: Turkmenistan OSCE Priorities]
For OSCE advisory work, Turkmenistan prioritizes large-scale geomembrane lining along Karakum Canal trunk lines and high-efficiency drip or pivot irrigation that preserves state-mandated agricultural employment.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 3A: Afghanistan Stance & Lines]
Afghanistan asserts sovereign rights over Amu Darya headwaters (Panj, Kokcha, Kunduz) for food security, expanding the Qosh Tepa Canal out of domestic need. Excluded from Soviet-era pacts (ICWC, Protocol 566), it sees downstream halt demands as unfair given low historical water use.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 3B: Afghanistan OSCE Priorities]
For OSCE advisory work, Afghanistan prioritizes water-saving irrigation technology, inclusion in regional hydrologic data sharing, and neutral multilateral confidence-building to lower cross-border friction.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 4A: Tajikistan Stance & Lines]
Tajikistan treats water mainly as a hydropower asset (Nurek, Rogun), storing summer water to generate winter electricity. It faces severe winter power shortages and seasonal friction with downstream cotton states seeking summer irrigation releases.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 4B: Tajikistan OSCE Priorities]
For OSCE advisory work, Tajikistan prioritizes water-energy compensation pacts (downstream winter energy support in exchange for summer irrigation releases) and green climate finance for high-efficiency hydro infrastructure.
---
[Dossier: Riparian States & OSCE | Sec 5: Kyrgyzstan Stance & OSCE]
Kyrgyzstan contributes minor upper Amu Darya tributary flows (and major Syr Darya flows), aligning with Tajikistan on hydropower and reservoir sovereignty. Facing Pamir-Alai glacial melt and tight budgets, it prioritizes hydrologic monitoring, climate early warning, and headwater ecosystem protection.
