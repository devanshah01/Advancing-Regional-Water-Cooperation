================================================================================
SUPPLEMENTARY DOSSIER: THE 5 RIPARIAN GOVERNMENTS, OSCE STRATEGIES, & RED LINES
================================================================================

1. UZBEKISTAN (The Downstream Agricultural Giant & Regional Convenor)
- Official Stance: Actively promotes regional integration, digitalization, and multilateral platforms (such as leading the International Fund for Saving the Aral Sea). Emphasizes that water scarcity threatens national security and economic growth (aiming to protect agricultural jobs in Karakalpakstan and Khorezm).
- Vulnerabilities & Red Lines: Highly vulnerable to upstream flow reductions because it sits at the tail-end of the Amu Darya. Views the uncoordinated construction of Afghanistan's Qosh Tepa Canal as a major security threat to its lower-basin water supply. 
- Policy Priorities for OSCE Advice: Infrastructure modernization, automated metering, canal lining, and integrating Afghanistan into formal transboundary dialogue.

2. TURKMENISTAN (The High-Consumption Lower Basin Consumer)
- Official Stance: Strictly guards state-mandated agricultural targets (cotton and wheat) and maintains a traditional policy of neutral, bilateral water diplomacy rather than loud multilateral confrontations.
- Vulnerabilities & Red Lines: Extremely dependent on the massive Karakum Canal. Experiences massive transit losses due to sandy desert seepage. Resists any international mechanism that would externally dictate internal volumetric cuts to its sovereign agricultural quotas.
- Policy Priorities for OSCE Advice: Financing large-scale geomembrane lining for the Karakum Canal trunk lines and introducing high-efficiency pivot and drip systems without disrupting state farm employment.

3. AFGHANISTAN (The Unrecognized Upstream Sovereign)
- Official Stance: Asserts sovereign rights over its domestic share of the Amu Darya headwaters (via the Panj, Kokcha, and Kunduz sub-basins) to feed its growing population and achieve food security. Built and continues expanding the Qosh Tepa Canal out of urgent domestic necessity.
- Vulnerabilities & Red Lines: Excluded from legacy Soviet-era legal frameworks (like the ICWC and Protocol 566). Views downstream pressure to halt infrastructure as unfair, noting that it used very little water historically compared to its neighbors.
- Policy Priorities for OSCE Advice: Technical assistance for water-saving irrigation within Afghanistan, inclusion in regional hydrologic data-sharing networks, and neutral multilateral confidence-building to prevent cross-border conflict.

4. TAJIKISTAN (The Upstream Hydropower Generator)
- Official Stance: Views water primarily as an energy asset (hydropower via structures like Nurek and the construction of Rogun) rather than just an agricultural fluid. Argue that they need to store water in summer and release it in winter for power generation.
- Vulnerabilities & Red Lines: Suffers severe winter energy deficits. Historically at odds with downstream cotton-farming nations that demand massive summer water releases for irrigation.
- Policy Priorities for OSCE Advice: Reviving multi-sectoral water-energy compensation pacts (where downstream states financially support upstream winter energy in exchange for summer irrigation flows) and securing green climate financing for high-efficiency dams.

5. KYRGYZSTAN (The Upper Mountain Source Contributor)
- Official Stance: Controls minor upper tributary flows of the Amu Darya basin system (alongside its major presence in the Syr Darya). Aligned closely with Tajikistan regarding prioritizing hydroelectric generation and upper-basin resource sovereignty.
- Vulnerabilities & Red Lines: Economically constrained, highly vulnerable to climate-induced glacial melting in the Pamir-Alai ranges, and protective of its sovereign right to construct and manage upstream reservoirs.
- Policy Priorities for OSCE Advice: Strengthening regional hydrologic monitoring, early-warning climate adaptation frameworks, and joint environmental protection of high-altitude headwater ecosystems.
