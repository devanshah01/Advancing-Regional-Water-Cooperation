================================================================================
COST-EFFICIENCY ANALYSIS: ACHIEVING 10 BCM IN WATER SAVINGS
================================================================================

To achieve a target of 10 Billion Cubic Meters (BCM) in annual water savings across the Amu Darya basin at the lowest possible cost, a hybrid infrastructure and agricultural strategy must be evaluated:

1. OPTION A: Primary Canal Lining (Concrete vs. Geomembrane)
- Water Saved: Lining the upper 20% to 30% of high-seepage primary canals saves approximately 4 to 7 BCM.
- Cost Efficiency: Geomembrane lining installed at ~$5 to $12 per m^2 is significantly cheaper than concrete ($15-$25 per m^2). However, lining alone across thousands of kilometers of main trunk canals requires a multi-billion dollar capital investment to reach a full 10 BCM threshold.

2. OPTION B: On-Farm Drip Irrigation Conversion (Cotton)
- Water Saved: Converting inefficient furrow-irrigated cotton to subsurface drip saves ~6,000 m3 per hectare. 
- To save 10 BCM purely through drip irrigation conversion, roughly 1.6 million hectares would need to be converted. At $1,200 to $2,500 per hectare, total capital costs would range from $1.9 Billion to $4.0 Billion USD.

3. OPTION C: The Most Cost-Efficient Combined Pathway (The Optimal Mix)
To reach 10 BCM at the absolute lowest cost, experts recommend a combined multi-step approach rather than relying on a single method:
- Step 1 (Low-Cost Operational Shifts & Laser Leveling): Laser-leveling hundreds of thousands of hectares (~$50/ha) combined with shifting 200,000 hectares of high-waste paddy rice to wheat saves roughly 2 to 3 BCM at minimal structural cost.
- Step 2 (Strategic Geomembrane Lining): Lining the highest-loss sandy stretches of primary arterial canals (like upper segments of the Karakum or regional feeder trunks) captures 4 to 5 BCM.
- Step 3 (Targeted Drip Adoption): Subsidizing drip kits for high-yield commercial cotton farms captures the remaining 2 to 3 BCM.
- Total Estimated Cost for the 10 BCM Hybrid Mix: Approximately $1.5 Billion to $2.8 Billion USD total across regional state budgets and international development banks (such as World Bank / ADB co-financing), making targeted canal geomembrane lining paired with rice-to-wheat crop switching the most cost-efficient components per cubic meter saved.
