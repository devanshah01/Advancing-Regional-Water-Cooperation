[Dossier: Aral Sea Geography & Shrinkage | Sec 1A: Original Dimensions]
Originally located in the northern desert of Central Asia within Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, the Aral Sea covered roughly 68,000 km2 (cited in text as 68 km2) with a volume of 1,053 to 1,064 km3. It ranked as the world's fourth-largest inland water body after the Caspian Sea, Lake Superior, and Lake Victoria.
---
[Dossier: Aral Sea Geography & Shrinkage | Sec 1B: Physical & Water Metrics]
Under natural conditions, the Aral Sea surface sat 53 meters above sea level (nearly 80 meters higher than the Caspian Sea), measuring 428 km long, 234 km wide, and up to 69 meters deep. Summer surface temperatures reached 26–30°C and dropped below 0°C in winter, with water transparency up to 25 meters and salinity steady around 9–11% (9–11 g/L).
---
[Dossier: Aral Sea Geography & Shrinkage | Sec 2: 1960–1990 Irrigation Drawdown]
In the last third of the 20th century, excessive irrigation withdrawals for newly developed virgin lands caused the Aral Sea to desiccate. From 1960 to 1990, basin irrigated land nearly doubled from 4.3 million to 8.2 million hectares, doubling water intake, dropping the sea level by over 20 meters, and retreating shores by 100–150 km near the Amu Darya and Syr Darya deltas.
---
[Dossier: Aral Sea Geography & Shrinkage | Sec 3A: 1990 Land Bridges & Lost Bays]
As the Aral Sea shrank, islands of the Akpetka archipelago merged with the mainland and intervening bays became salt marshes. By 1990, Kokaral Island merged with the mainland, eliminating Saryshiganak Bay and the Berg Strait (Bay of Berg) that had connected the Big (Large) Aral Sea and Small (North) Aral Sea.
---
[Dossier: Aral Sea Geography & Shrinkage | Sec 3B: Split of the Great Sea]
Following the 1990 separation of the Big and Small Aral Seas, remaining islands continued expanding and merging with one another and the mainland. Within a decade, these coalescing landmasses split the Great (South) Aral Sea into two distinct western and eastern basins.
