[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 1: Canal Lining]
Upgrading high-loss earthen arterial channels (such as the Karakum Canal and primary Uzbek feeder networks) with concrete slabs or sealed HDPE geomembranes cuts transit seepage that loses 30-40% of diverted water. Lining the first 20% of trunk canals saves ~4 to 7 BCM annually; concrete costs $15-$25/m2 and installed geomembranes cost $5-$12/m2.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 2: Cotton Drip Irrigation]
Transitioning commercial cotton fields in Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan from open-furrow flooding to pressurized surface or subsurface drip lines reduces water use from 12,000 m3/ha to 4,500-6,000 m3/ha (a 50-60% reduction) while maintaining or raising lint yields. Complete on-farm drip kits and filtration units cost $1,200 to $2,500 per hectare.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 3: Crop Switching from Rice]
Replacing paddy rice (15,000-20,000+ m3/ha) in hyper-arid lower basin areas like Khorezm and Karakalpakstan with winter wheat, pulses, or drought-tolerant fodder (4,000-6,000 m3/ha) saves 60-70% of water per hectare. Structural cost is near zero, relying instead on state agricultural quota reforms, subsidy shifts, and farmer extension programs.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 4: SCADA & Digital Metering]
Installing real-time electronic flow meters, automated gates, and telemetry sensors at transboundary choke points and inter-farm canal gates eliminates human error and water theft, cutting distribution losses by 12-15%. Major automated nodes cost $5,000 to $15,000 each, with regional modernization packages totaling $20M to $50M.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 5: Laser Land Leveling]
Laser-guided grading flattens agricultural plots before seasonal planting to prevent uneven pooling and low-spot over-watering, cutting total irrigation demand by 20-25% immediately. The one-time service costs $40 to $80 per hectare and typically pays for itself within a single growing season through water and fuel savings.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 6: Drainage Water Blending]
Conjunctive use pumps, treats, and blends brackish collector-drainage water with fresh river water at mixing stations. This taps into over 25 BCM of annual basin drainage discharge to ease summer river extraction pressure, costing $500,000 to $2M per regional blending and salinity-reduction plant.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 7: VFD Pumping Upgrades]
Overhauling aging 1970s Soviet-era hydro-pumps (which lift Amu Darya water onto high steppes such as Karshi) with modern Variable Frequency Drive (VFD) motors cuts energy intensity by 15-25% and prevents chronic delivery breakdowns. Major pumping station overhauls cost $200,000 to $1.5M per station.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 8: Water-Energy Pacts]
Upstream-downstream institutional pacts allow Uzbekistan and Turkmenistan to finance winter energy for Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan so upstream dams store summer water for irrigation and release it for winter power. This addresses a mismatch costing $4.5B+ yearly, requiring ~$10M to $25M in diplomatic and facilitation funds (such as World Bank CAWEP).
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 9: Riparian & Wetland Buffers]
Restoring natural reed beds, wetlands, and riparian forests along the lower Amu Darya and Aral Sea delta bio-filters agricultural chemical and salt runoff before it reaches downstream towns while reducing open-pool evaporation. Ecological restoration and community reed planting cost $1,000 to $3,000 per hectare.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya 10 Solutions | Sol 10: Water Users Associations]
Decentralizing canal governance from state ministries to farmer-run Water Users Associations (WUAs) with volumetric water pricing rather than flat rates cuts tertiary farm-ditch waste by 15-30%. Initial institutional capacity building and legal setup cost roughly $50,000 to $150,000 per district association.
