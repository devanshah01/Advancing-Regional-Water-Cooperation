[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 1: Concrete Lining Specs]
Concrete canal lining (cast-in-place or precast slabs) uses a 50mm to 100mm layer of reinforced or unreinforced concrete over canal beds and slopes. It requires expansion joints for extreme Central Asian temperature swings, reduces seepage by 85% to 95%, and lasts 30 to 50 years with freeze-thaw and crack maintenance.
---
[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 1: Concrete Lining Costs]
Concrete canal lining costs roughly $15 to $25 per square meter installed (covering excavation grading, sub-base prep, pouring, and joint sealing), plus $2 to $4 per square meter per year in ongoing crack and joint maintenance.
---
[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 2: Geomembrane Specs]
Geomembrane canal liners use 1.0mm to 2.0mm industrial plastic sheets (HDPE, LLDPE, or PVC) laid over the canal and typically buried under earth or gravel to block UV rays and livestock damage. They achieve near-100% seepage reduction and last 20 to 40 years depending on UV protection and burial depth.
---
[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 2: Geomembrane Costs]
Geomembrane canal liners cost $1.00 to $4.00 per square meter for materials alone (varying by thickness and polymer grade) and $5.00 to $12.00 per square meter fully installed (including trenching, anchoring, and seam welding), making them far cheaper than full concrete lining.
---
[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 3: Composite Lining]
Geomembrane-concrete composite lining places a thin plastic geomembrane underneath protective shotcrete or precast concrete blocks. It achieves 100% seepage reduction, lasts 40 to 60+ years by armoring the waterproof seal against wear, and costs $18 to $30 per square meter upfront.
---
[Dossier: Canal Lining Specs & Cost | Sec 4: Soil-Cement & Earth]
Compacted earth and soil-cement lining mixes local canal soil with cement or heavily compacts clay layers without importing costly materials. It cuts seepage by 50% to 70%, lasts 10 to 20 years (vulnerable to high-velocity erosion), and costs $3 to $8 per square meter for budget-constrained secondary or tertiary canals.
