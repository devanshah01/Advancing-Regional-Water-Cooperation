[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 1: Natural Flow Volume]
The Amu Darya generates an average annual natural flow of roughly 70 to 80 cubic kilometers (km3), or billion cubic meters (BCM), across its upstream headwaters—primarily the Vakhsh and Panj rivers in Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 1: Lower Basin Delivery]
By the time the Amu Darya passes Turkmenistan into Uzbekistan and Karakalpakstan, active surface flow drops to 35 to 50 BCM per year due to upstream abstractions, evaporation, and transit seepage.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 2: Earthen Canal Losses]
Over 40% to 50% of water diverted from the Amu Darya main stem and major arterial networks (such as the Karakum Canal and inter-farm channels) is lost in unlined earthen canals before ever reaching farm fields.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 2: Primary vs. Secondary Seepage]
Secondary and tertiary farm ditches lose up to 40-50% of flow by percentage. However, massive primary arterial canals (like the head and middle sections of the Karakum Canal) cause huge absolute water loss due to high flow volume, wide wetted perimeters, and sandy desert soil infiltration.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 2: Total Annual System Loss]
Across the Central Asian canal grid fed by the Amu Darya, roughly 50 to 60 BCM of water is diverted annually, and approximately 20 to 25 BCM is lost each year to unlined bed seepage and direct evaporation.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 3: Main Canal Network Scale]
The primary network of major canals branching off the Amu Darya spans thousands of kilometers—including the Karakum Canal (over 1,300 km) and major primary Uzbek collector and diversion trunks.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 3: First 20% Canal Physics]
The first 20% of major diversion canals closest to river headworks carry the highest water volume under peak hydrostatic pressure. Unlined sandy and loam beds in these upper zones lose roughly 25% to 35% of passing water through deep percolation into desert sands.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 3: Lining First 20% Efficiency]
Lining the first 20% of primary Amu Darya trunk canals with concrete or low-permeability geomembranes increases transit efficiency in those upper segments from roughly 70% up to 95%.
---
[Dossier: Amu Darya Infrastructure | Sec 3: Lining First 20% Savings Math]
Lining the first 20% of primary Amu Darya diversion canals saves approximately 4 to 7 cubic kilometers (BCM / billion cubic meters) of water annually—comparable to a small country's annual water use or enough to revitalize major sections of the Aral Sea delta.
